# /client-status-update — Client Status Update Drafter

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. It has not been reviewed by a licensed attorney. Do not send this email without your independent review and approval. This is not legal advice.

Draft a personalized client update email in the attorney's voice, drawing from local matter files and recent email history.

**This skill reads files and email. It does not send anything.** Before any draft is sent, you must confirm explicitly: "Yes, send it."

## Invocation

```
/client-status-update [matter folder path or client name]
```

If no argument is provided, ask the attorney which client or matter this update is for.

---

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Locate the matter folder

Use the Filesystem connector to find the matter folder. Common paths:
- `~/Matters/[ClientName]/`
- `~/Documents/Cases/[ClientName]/`
- Ask if uncertain.

Within the matter folder, look for:
- A `matter.md` or `intake.md` file (structured case summary)
- Recent notes, filings, or hearing summaries (any `.md`, `.txt`, or `.docx` files)
- A `voice.md` or prior email drafts (to capture the attorney's tone)

### Step 2 — Scan recent Gmail for context

Use the Gmail connector to search for recent emails involving the client:
- Search: `to:[client email] OR from:[client email]` (last 30 days)
- Look for: the most recent substantive update sent to the client, any client questions that remain unanswered, and any recent developments (opposing counsel correspondence, court notifications).

Note the date and subject of the last update sent so the new email picks up from there.

### Step 3 — Ask the attorney what happened

If the matter files don't contain a clear description of the triggering development, ask:

> "What is the update you'd like to communicate? (e.g., 'We filed the complaint today,' 'The deposition is scheduled for June 3,' 'We received a $25,000 settlement offer.')"

### Step 4 — Draft the email

Draft a plain-language client update email using the following structure:

```
Subject: Update on Your Matter — [Brief Description]

[Greeting],

I wanted to update you on where things stand with your case.

[1–2 sentences describing what happened or was completed]

[1–2 sentences explaining what it means for the client in plain language — no jargon]

[Next step: what happens next and any action required from the client, with dates if known]

[Closing — professional and personal, matching the attorney's existing tone]

[Attorney name]
[Firm name]
[Contact info if present in matter folder]
```

**Voice rules:**
- Mirror the tone of prior emails found in Gmail or the matter folder.
- Avoid legal jargon unless the attorney's existing emails use it.
- Keep it under 200 words unless the development is complex.
- Never speculate about outcomes or make promises about the case.

### Step 5 — Present draft and request confirmation

Present the full draft email and then ask:

> "Does this look right? Would you like any changes before I proceed? Once you say 'yes, send it' or 'yes, draft it,' I'll use Gmail to create the draft/send the message."

Do not invoke Gmail to send or create a draft until the attorney explicitly confirms.

### Step 6 — Send or draft via Gmail

After confirmation:
- If the attorney says "send it": use the Gmail connector to send the email.
- If the attorney says "save as draft" or "draft it": use the Gmail connector to create a draft.
- Confirm completion: "Done — email sent to [client name] at [address]" or "Draft saved to your Gmail Drafts folder."
