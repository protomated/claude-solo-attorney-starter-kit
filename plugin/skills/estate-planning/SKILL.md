# /estate-planning — Estate Planning Document Assembly Skill

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. It has not been reviewed by a licensed attorney. Do not have a client review, sign, or execute any of these documents without your independent review and approval. Estate planning documents have strict state-specific execution requirements (witnesses, notarization, self-proving affidavits) that this skill does not verify. This is not legal advice.

Estate planning attorneys draft near-identical document sets repeatedly — a basic will, healthcare power of attorney, financial power of attorney, and HIPAA authorization, typically 2–3 hours of assembly per client. This skill populates all four consistently from one intake pass, using the attorney's own state-specific templates when one is on file. If no template exists for a document type, it drafts from a generic, non-state-specific placeholder and says so plainly — it never claims the placeholder is compliant with any state's execution formalities. Every document lists the required fields it still needs before the attorney should consider it complete.

**This skill reads files only. It does not send anything. No file is written without your explicit confirmation.**

## Invocation

```
/estate-planning [matter folder path]
```

Supported documents: **basic will**, **healthcare power of attorney** (healthcare proxy / medical POA), **financial power of attorney** (durable/general POA), **HIPAA authorization**. Trusts, pour-over wills, beneficiary deeds, state funding checklists, and signing-ceremony scheduling are out of scope for this free-tier skill.

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Gather estate-planning intake data

Use the Filesystem connector to find the matter folder, then look for, in order:
1. `estate-planning-intake.md` — a dedicated intake file if one exists.
2. `intake-summary.md` — the anchor file from `/intake-summary`, for baseline client name/contact/address info. It will not contain most estate-specific fields below.
3. Raw notes pasted directly by the attorney.

Cross-reference what you find against the field groups below. For any field not found in files, ask the attorney directly rather than guessing or leaving it blank silently — do not infer relationships, dollar amounts, or names. This step should take a few minutes for an attorney who already has intake notes on hand — the setup target for this skill is about 10 minutes total, including template review in Step 3.

**Family:**
- Client full legal name, address, date of birth
- Marital status; spouse's full legal name if married
- Children (full names, dates of birth, note which are currently minors)
- Other dependents

**Assets (high-level, for drafting references — this skill does not fund a trust or retitle anything):**
- Real property
- Bank and investment accounts
- Retirement accounts and their current beneficiary designations
- Life insurance and its current beneficiary designations
- Significant personal property
- Business interests

**Beneficiaries and fiduciaries:**
- Primary beneficiaries (names, relationship, share or specific bequest)
- Contingent beneficiaries
- Executor — primary and alternate (name, address)
- Guardian for minor children — primary and alternate (name, address) — **required whenever any child is currently a minor; there is no substitute for this field**
- Agent for financial POA — primary and alternate (name, address, phone)
- Agent for healthcare POA — primary and alternate (name, address, phone)

**Healthcare wishes:**
- Life-sustaining treatment preferences (a general statement for the healthcare POA — this is not a full advance directive/living will; note that gap to the attorney if their practice also handles those separately)
- Organ donation preference
- Primary physician, if known

**HIPAA:**
- Authorized recipients of protected health information (names, relationship — often overlaps with the healthcare agent, but list separately since HIPAA access and healthcare decision-making authority are legally distinct)

Mark any field you cannot find or the attorney cannot yet supply as `[Not provided]`. Do not fabricate a name, date, or asset.

### Step 2 — Check for a hard blocker: minors without a named guardian

Before moving to templates, check the family data from Step 1: if any child is currently a minor and no guardian (primary or alternate) has been named, flag this immediately and prominently — do not wait until the Step 7 missing-fields report to surface it:

> "[Client]'s children include [minor children names], who are currently minors. I don't have a guardian named for them. This is a required field for the will — who would you like to name as primary and alternate guardian?"

You may proceed to draft with this field marked `[CONFIRM WITH ATTORNEY — GUARDIAN REQUIRED]` if the attorney wants to keep moving, but the flag must reappear first in the Step 7 missing-fields report — never let it get buried among lower-stakes gaps like an unconfirmed phone number.

### Step 3 — Locate the attorney's own templates

Before drafting, check whether the attorney has their own state-specific template for each document type — this skill fills in the attorney's own wording whenever one is available, and only falls back to a generic placeholder when it is not. Use the Filesystem connector to check, in order, for each document type:

1. **The matter folder itself** (a matter-specific override, if the attorney saved one there — check `drafts/will/` or `drafts/poa/`, or the matter root).
2. **A `templates/` folder at the top level of the connected Filesystem workspace** (e.g. `~/Matters/templates/`) — the standard place to keep templates reused across every matter.

Match filenames case-insensitively per document type:

| Document | Filename keywords to match |
|---|---|
| Will | `will`, `last will`, `testament` (do not match `living will` here — that term usually refers to an advance directive, not this document; ask the attorney if a file matches only `living will`) |
| Healthcare POA | `healthcare poa`, `healthcare power of attorney`, `medical poa`, `medical power of attorney`, `healthcare proxy`, `advance directive`, `ahcd` |
| Financial POA | `financial poa`, `financial power of attorney`, `durable power of attorney`, `general power of attorney` |
| HIPAA authorization | `hipaa`, `hipaa authorization`, `hipaa release` |

Extensions: `.docx`, `.doc`, `.md`, `.txt`, `.rtf`. Note every match per document type as a candidate — don't read contents yet. A document type with no candidate falls back to its built-in placeholder in Step 6B. Track this per document type independently — it's normal for an attorney to have a will template on file but not a HIPAA template, and each document type resolves separately.

### Step 4 — Select and read each template

For each document type with one or more candidates from Step 3:

- **One candidate:** read it.
- **Multiple candidates:** ask the attorney which one to use.

Run two checks per template before drafting from it:

**Extraction check** — if the Filesystem connector returns no usable text (common with some `.docx`/`.doc` files), do not guess at the content and do not silently fall back to the placeholder. Tell the attorney:
> "I found `[filename]` for [document type] but couldn't extract readable text from it. Could you paste the template text directly, or save a `.md` or `.txt` copy in the same location?"

**Completeness check** — echo back the section headings you found and ask the attorney to confirm nothing was dropped or reordered:
> "I'm using `[filename]` as your [document type] template. It has these sections: [list headings in order]. Does that match your template, or is anything missing?"

Only draft from a given template once the attorney confirms it, or once they've supplied usable pasted text for it. Document types without a confirmed template use the built-in placeholder in Step 6B.

### Step 5 — Check required fields per document type

Cross-reference the intake data from Step 1 against the required-fields table below for each of the four document types. List what's missing — this is the skill's core value: catching gaps before the attorney spends review time finding them.

| Document | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Will | Client full legal name and address; marital status; executor (name + address) and alternate; guardian for minor children and alternate (**hard blocker if any child is currently a minor** — see Step 2); residuary beneficiary(ies); any specific bequests |
| Healthcare POA | Client full legal name; healthcare agent (name, address, phone) and alternate; scope of authority to be granted; general statement of life-sustaining-treatment wishes |
| Financial POA | Client full legal name; agent (name, address, phone) and alternate; powers granted (real property, banking, business operations, gifts, etc.); durability (effective immediately vs. springing on incapacity); any termination conditions |
| HIPAA authorization | Client full legal name; authorized recipient(s) of health information (name + relationship); scope of records covered; expiration or duration |

Build a consolidated missing-fields list, grouped by document, to show the attorney before the drafts (Step 7) — do not draft silently around a gap.

### Step 6 — Draft each document

Draft all four document types. For each one, use whichever of the following applies:

**Step 6A — Using the attorney's own template (if confirmed in Step 4):**

Use the attorney's own section structure, headings, and clause language as the base. Fill in the variable fields — names, dates, addresses, beneficiary and fiduciary designations, granted powers — from the intake data gathered in Step 1. Do not add any clause or section that isn't already present in the attorney's template; if their template appears to be missing something the placeholder below would normally cover, flag it in Step 7 rather than inserting it yourself.

**Step 6B — Built-in placeholder (no usable attorney template found for that document type):**

> **These four placeholders are intentionally generic.** They do not reflect any state's statutory will formalities, self-proving affidavit language, notarization requirements, or witness-count rules — those vary by state and change over time. Never let a client sign a placeholder-drafted document without the attorney first replacing it with (or verifying it against) their jurisdiction's compliant form.

**Basic Will placeholder:**
```

⚠️ GENERIC PLACEHOLDER — NOT STATE-SPECIFIC. Replace with your jurisdiction's compliant will form before use. Witness and notarization requirements are NOT included and vary by state.

ARTICLE I — FAMILY
I am [married to / not married to] [Spouse Full Legal Name, if applicable]. I have the following children: [list names and dates of birth, or "no children"].

ARTICLE II — APPOINTMENT OF EXECUTOR
I appoint [Executor Name] of [Executor Address] as Executor of this Will. If [Executor Name] is unable or unwilling to serve, I appoint [Alternate Executor Name] as successor Executor. My Executor shall serve without bond to the extent permitted by law.

[Include only if any child is currently a minor — if none, state "Not applicable — no minor children."]
If I have minor children at the time of my death, I appoint [Guardian Name] of [Guardian Address] as guardian. If unable or unwilling to serve, I appoint [Alternate Guardian Name] as successor guardian.

ARTICLE IV — SPECIFIC BEQUESTS
[List each specific bequest, or state "None."]

ARTICLE V — RESIDUARY ESTATE

```

**Drafting rules (apply to both 6A and 6B):**
- Fill brackets from the intake data gathered in Step 1; leave any unfilled bracket visibly marked `[CONFIRM WITH ATTORNEY]`.
- Never introduce a clause, section, or term not already present in the source (attorney's template or placeholder) — flag anything missing in Step 7 instead of adding it yourself.
- Do not draft execution/witness/notarization language for any document — state requirements vary and this skill does not track them; always leave the `[⚠️ WITNESS/NOTARIZATION BLOCK OMITTED]` marker (or attorney template's own such section) intact.
- If minor children are present and no guardian was provided, do not silently omit Article III of the will — draft it with `[CONFIRM WITH ATTORNEY — GUARDIAN REQUIRED]` per the Step 2 flag.

### Step 7 — Present all four drafts and the missing-fields report

Show the attorney all four drafts together with the consolidated missing-fields list from Step 5, with the Step 2 guardian blocker (if applicable) called out first:

> "Here are the four drafts. Before any of these are ready for client review: [list missing/unconfirmed fields, grouped by document — guardian-required flag first if applicable]. Would you like any changes?"

### Step 8 — Save to matter folder (optional, with confirmation)

Propose destinations consistent with the folder structure `/new-matter-organizer` creates for estate planning matters:

> "Would you like me to save these in [matter folder path]?
> - Will → `drafts/will/will-draft-[date].md`
> - Healthcare POA → `drafts/poa/healthcare-poa-draft-[date].md`
> - Financial POA → `drafts/poa/financial-poa-draft-[date].md`
> - HIPAA authorization → `drafts/poa/hipaa-authorization-draft-[date].md`"

Do not write any file without explicit confirmation. The attorney may confirm all four, a subset, or none.

## What This Skill Does Not Do

- It does not draft a trust, pour-over will, or beneficiary deed, and it does not produce a state funding checklist or schedule a signing ceremony — these are out of scope for the free tier; ask about a custom build if the client's plan needs to avoid probate or needs full estate funding support.
- It does not determine your state's execution formalities (witness count, notarization, self-proving affidavit language) — it leaves those sections marked for you to complete.
- It does not confirm the placeholder templates comply with any state's statutory requirements — the placeholders exist so you have a starting structure, not a filing-ready document.
- It does not fund the estate plan — retitling assets or updating beneficiary designations on retirement accounts and life insurance is a separate, attorney-directed follow-up step.
- It does not assess testamentary capacity or advise on will-contest risk.
- It does not replace review by a licensed attorney admitted in the client's state.
