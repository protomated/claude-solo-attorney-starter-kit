# /flat-fee-calculator — AI-Adjusted Flat-Fee Repricing Calculator

> ⚠️ AI-ASSISTED DRAFT — ATTORNEY REVIEW REQUIRED
> This output was generated by an AI assistant. It is a pricing and business model, not legal advice and not an opinion on what your state bar's fee-reasonableness rules permit. Review the numbers and assumptions before using them to set client pricing.

Hourly billing structurally punishes AI-driven efficiency gains: if a task that used to take two hours now takes forty minutes, hourly billing hands the client an unrequested discount instead of letting the firm capture any of the time saved. This skill builds a revenue model — current hourly, AI-accelerated hourly, and several flat-fee scenarios — from numbers the attorney supplies, and outputs a CSV file the attorney can open in Excel, Google Sheets, or Numbers.

**This skill reads files only unless the attorney confirms a save. It does not change any billing system, rate, or client agreement.**

## Invocation

```
/flat-fee-calculator [task or matter type]
/flat-fee-calculator "standard NDA review"
/flat-fee-calculator "uncontested divorce document package"
```

## Workflow

### Step 1 — Gather inputs

Ask the attorney for, or infer from the argument:

1. **Task or matter type being modeled** — specific enough to have a consistent time estimate (e.g., "initial consultation memo," not "family law" broadly).
2. **Current hourly rate** ($/hour).
3. **Typical time before using AI tools** (hours, e.g. "2.5 hrs").
4. **Typical time after using AI tools** (hours) — including this kit's own skills where relevant (e.g., `/intake-summary`, `/billing-narrative`, `/engagement-letter` cutting drafting time).
5. **Matter volume** — how many of this task/matter type per month or year. Ask which period and keep the model consistent with it.

**Optional — grounding the estimate in real data:** if a matter folder or workspace is attached, use the Filesystem connector to search for saved `billing-narrative` output or time entries mentioning the task type keyword, to sanity-check the attorney's before/after estimates. This is a cross-check, not a replacement — always ask the attorney to confirm or adjust the final numbers before modeling. If nothing is found, proceed directly with the attorney's estimates.

If any of the five inputs is missing, ask for it directly. Do not assume or invent a rate, time, or volume.

### Step 2 — Compute the baseline and the leak

Using the confirmed inputs:

- **Baseline hourly revenue** (per matter, pre-AI): `hourly_rate × hours_before`
- **AI-accelerated hourly revenue** (per matter, staying on hourly billing): `hourly_rate × hours_after`
- **The leak** (per matter): `baseline hourly revenue − AI-accelerated hourly revenue` — this is the amount handed to the client as an unrequested discount for every matter of this type, under hourly billing.
- **Annualized leak**: `leak per matter × matter volume` (normalized to a 12-month figure regardless of the period the attorney gave in Step 1).

### Step 3 — Model flat-fee price points

Present three flat-fee scenarios per matter. Do not present these as recommendations of what the attorney's rate "should" be — they are candidate reference points for the attorney to weigh against their own judgment of client price sensitivity and local market rates.

| Scenario | Formula | What it represents |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Break-even flat fee** | `hourly_rate × hours_before` | Same per-matter revenue as pre-AI hourly billing. Firm keeps 100% of the efficiency gain; client pays the same as before. Of the three, this always has the highest client-pushback risk — it is mathematically always the highest-revenue scenario whenever AI saves any time at all. |
| **B — Split the savings (50/50)** | `(hourly_rate × hours_after) + 0.5 × (hourly_rate × hours_before − hourly_rate × hours_after)` | Client and firm each keep half the time saved. A common, defensible middle position. Always below A; whether it lands above or below C depends on how much time AI actually saved (see note below the table). |
| **C — Value-conscious flat fee** | `hourly_rate × hours_after × 1.25` | A 25% margin over AI-accelerated hourly cost — priced closer to actual time spent, positioned as a modest premium for outcome certainty. Always above the status-quo hourly revenue; whether it lands above or below B depends on the size of the AI time savings (see note below the table). |

For each scenario, also compute:
- **Per-matter revenue** (the formula result)
- **Annual revenue** at the given matter volume (normalized to 12 months)
- **Effective realized hourly rate**: `scenario per-matter revenue ÷ hours_after` — shows the attorney what they're actually earning per hour worked under each scenario, for comparison against their nominal hourly rate.

### Step 4 — Build the comparison table

Assemble all scenarios plus the two hourly baselines into one table:

| Approach | Per-Matter Revenue | Annual Revenue (× volume) | Effective $/hr Worked |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stay hourly, pre-AI time | [baseline] | [× volume] | [= nominal rate — by definition, billing hourly always nets the nominal rate per hour worked] |
| Stay hourly, AI-accelerated (status quo leak) | [AI-accelerated hourly] | [× volume] | [= nominal rate — same rate as above; the leak shows up as lower *revenue* per matter, not a lower rate] |
| Flat fee — A: Break-even | [Scenario A] | [× volume] | [computed value] |
| Flat fee — B: Split savings 50/50 | [Scenario B] | [× volume] | [computed value] |
| Flat fee — C: Value-conscious | [Scenario C] | [× volume] | [computed value] |

Compute each flat-fee scenario's actual effective $/hr and sort the three by that value in the presented table — do not assume a fixed ranking. A > B always holds when AI saves any time at all, but whether B or C is higher depends on how much time AI actually saves: B beats C only when `hours_after ÷ hours_before < 2/3` (roughly a 33%+ time reduction). For smaller efficiency gains, C can land above B. Show the attorney the numbers you actually computed, not an assumed order.

### Step 5 — Draft the CSV model

Produce a CSV with these columns so the attorney can open it directly in Excel/Sheets and adjust assumptions themselves:

```csv
Scenario,Hourly Rate,Hours per Matter,Matter Volume (Annual),Per-Matter Revenue,Annual Revenue,Effective $/hr Worked
Stay Hourly - Pre-AI Time,[rate],[hours_before],[volume],[=rate*hours_before],[=per-matter*volume],[=rate]
Stay Hourly - AI-Accelerated (Status Quo),[rate],[hours_after],[volume],[=rate*hours_after],[=per-matter*volume],[=rate]
Flat Fee A - Break-Even,,[hours_after],[volume],[Scenario A],[=per-matter*volume],[=A/hours_after]
Flat Fee B - Split Savings 50/50,,[hours_after],[volume],[Scenario B],[=per-matter*volume],[=B/hours_after]
Flat Fee C - Value-Conscious,,[hours_after],[volume],[Scenario C],[=per-matter*volume],[=C/hours_after]
```

Fill every bracket with the computed number — do not leave formulas as text in the CSV, write the resolved values so the file opens cleanly in a spreadsheet program. Round currency to the nearest dollar and hours to one decimal place.

### Step 6 — Present the model and the narrative summary

Show, in this order:
1. The comparison table from Step 4.
2. A short narrative: the size of the annualized leak from Step 2, and a plain statement of the trade-off between the three flat-fee scenarios (revenue recovery vs. client-pushback risk).
3. One caveat the attorney should weigh before repricing: client price sensitivity for this task/matter type, whether the state bar's fee-reasonableness rules apply differently to flat vs. hourly fees, and that any repriced engagement needs a corresponding update to the engagement letter's fee section (`/engagement-letter`).

> "Here's the model for [task/matter type]. Would you like me to save this as a CSV you can open in Excel or Sheets?"

### Step 7 — Save to workspace (optional, with confirmation)

Propose a destination, e.g. `pricing/[task-type]-repricing-model-[date].csv` at the top level of the connected Filesystem workspace (not inside a single matter folder, since this model applies across matters of a given type).

Do not write any file without explicit confirmation.

## What This Skill Does Not Do

- It does not set your rates or tell you which scenario to choose — it models the trade-offs; you decide based on your market and client relationships.
- It does not verify your state bar's fee-reasonableness or flat-fee-specific rules — confirm those separately before repricing.
- It does not update any engagement letter, billing system, or client agreement — use `/engagement-letter` to draft updated fee language once you've picked a price point.
- It does not track actual realized time or revenue after repricing — it is a one-time (or periodically re-run) planning model, not a time tracker.
- It does not account for costs, overhead, or non-billable time — it models fee revenue only.
